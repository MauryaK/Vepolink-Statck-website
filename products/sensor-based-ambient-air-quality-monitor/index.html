01 · Overview

A full monitoring station, on a single pole.

A sensor-based continuous ambient air quality monitoring station that delivers real-time, precise and reliable data for industrial, urban and environmental applications - without the footprint, shelter or utilities of a conventional analyzer cabin.

Advanced electrochemical gas cells and optical laser particulate sensing sit alongside a full weather station in one compact, weather-resistant housing. Low power draw and an efficient solar system let the station run off-grid.

02 · How it works

Sensing, weather & data path.

Two independent sensing technologies cover gases and particles, a meteorological head puts every reading in context, and an IoT data path carries results from the pole to your dashboard.

Electrochemical

Catalytic reaction

High-precision electrochemical cells measure any six gases from the library, with 15-30 second response.

Optical laser scattering

Particle size classification

A laser beam resolves PM1, PM2.5 and PM10 by scattering, updating in as little as 4 seconds.

Meteorology

Integrated weather head

Co-located wind, temperature, humidity, pressure and solar readings put pollutant data in context.

Connectivity

IoT data path

Measurements are pushed to the cloud every minute over Ethernet, RS-485 or 2G/3G/4G, with remote access and alerts.

06 · FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

What is the working principle?

Electrochemical catalytic cells measure gases, while optical laser scattering measures particulate matter. A co-located weather station adds environmental context.

Which parameters can it measure?

PM1, PM2.5 and PM10, plus any six gases configured from CO, CO₂, NO₂, SO₂, O₃, H₂S, NH₃, CH₄, TVOC and HCl.

Is it suitable for continuous monitoring?

Yes. It is designed for real-time operation and uploads station data to the cloud every minute.

Can it run at remote sites?

Yes. Solar power, lithium backup and 2G/3G/4G connectivity support off-grid deployment.

At what height should it be mounted?

Approximately 2 m above ground, with the inlet between 2 and 4 m where site conditions allow.

How does it integrate with SCADA?

Ethernet, RS-485 and MODBUS RTU slave connectivity support plant networks and existing SCADA systems.
